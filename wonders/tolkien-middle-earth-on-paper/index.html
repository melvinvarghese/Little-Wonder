Middle-earth, Before It Was a Map in Your Mind

1 min read ✧ A small discovery

A fictional world can be drawn as well as written. The Bodleian Libraries’ Tolkien exhibition brought together draft manuscripts, illustrations, and maps made for The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings.

Those materials reveal an author at work across several forms. A map gives geography a visible shape; an illustration can test the look of a place; a manuscript carries the changing sequence of words. Tolkien’s papers also included early abstract paintings, stories written for his children, and private letters, placing published fantasy beside the ordinary traces of a working life.

Seeing these objects together complicates the idea that a world appears fully formed in a reader’s imagination. The printed books are the doorway, but sketches and drafts show some of the routes by which their landscapes took shape. The archive preserves not only the world, but ways of making it.

Think about this

A map can guide us through an imagined land; a draft can show how the land was first found.
