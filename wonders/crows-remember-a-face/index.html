The Crow That Remembers Your Face

1 min read ✧ A small discovery

A crow may spot you once and remember you long after the moment has passed.

In a University of Washington study, researchers wore a distinctive mask while catching, banding, and releasing wild American crows. Later, crows scolded people wearing that same face, even when the person was alone and the original encounter was over. The birds that had been caught learned directly; others appeared to pick up the warning by watching the commotion.

The lesson traveled through crow communities, including from parents to young birds. Over five years at one site, scolding spread at least 1.2 kilometers from where the research began. That is not a human-style story in a bird’s mind; it is evidence that crows can recognize a particular face and learn which ones matter from one another.

Think about this

Next time a crow watches from a branch, consider what details it may be noticing—and what its neighbors might learn, too.
