# Research notes: The Unanswered

**Scope:** Ten open questions, checked for this October 2026 task against institutional, scholarly, and reputable reporting sources. Sources cited below were searched and fetched in full, except where a caveat says otherwise; article copy distinguishes established observations from unresolved explanations. The DESI status is explicitly current through its April 2026 update; no claims are made about later unpublished results.

1. **Dark matter identity**
   - NASA's overview states that dark matter's gravitational effects are inferred from galaxy dynamics, gravitational lensing, and galaxy-cluster observations; its physical nature remains unknown and candidate particles are unconfirmed.
   - Sources: [NASA Science — Dark Matter](https://science.nasa.gov/dark-matter/); [DESI Collaboration — April 2026 mapping milestone](https://www.desi.lbl.gov/2026/04/15/desi-reaches-mapping-milestone-surpassing-expectations/).

2. **Dark energy and acceleration**
   - NASA reports cosmic acceleration and frames dark energy as its unknown cause. Fermilab's March 2025 report says DESI's first three years, when combined with other probes, hinted at evolution but remained below the 5-sigma discovery threshold; DESI's April 2026 completion update says the full-survey analysis was still in processing, with first results expected in 2027. Avoid treating hints as discovery.
   - Sources: [NASA Science — Dark Energy](https://science.nasa.gov/dark-energy/); [Fermilab — New DESI results strengthen hints dark energy may evolve](https://news.fnal.gov/2025/03/new-desi-results-strengthen-hints-that-dark-energy-may-evolve/); [DESI Collaboration — April 2026 mapping milestone](https://www.desi.lbl.gov/2026/04/15/desi-reaches-mapping-milestone-surpassing-expectations/).

3. **Origin of life**
   - The scholarly review treats broad chemical-to-biological pathways as researchable but states that the detailed mechanisms, materials, and historic sequence remain unresolved; it lists open questions about replicating networks and their emergence. Lab demonstrations of chemical components are not a reconstruction of the first living system.
   - Source: [Pross, “The origin of life: what we know, what we can know and what we will never know,” Interface Focus / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3718341/).

4. **Conscious experience**
   - The Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy defines the “hard problem” as accounting for why physical processes are accompanied by subjective experience, while distinguishing that from experimentally tractable questions about function and neural correlates. The article acknowledges multiple live philosophical positions, not a settled scientific verdict.
   - Sources: [Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy — The Hard Problem of Consciousness](https://iep.utm.edu/hard-problem-of-conciousness/); [Berent, 2023, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10449398/).

5. **Matter–antimatter asymmetry**
   - CERN says CP violation is known in the Standard Model, but the known sources are insufficient to explain the cosmic matter excess; LHCb's 2024 reported measurement was consistent with previous studies and showed no evidence for CP violation in the particular neutral-D-meson oscillation studied.
   - Sources: [CERN — Searching for New Asymmetry Between Matter and Antimatter](https://home.cern/searching-new-asymmetry-between-matter-and-antimatter/);.

6. **Black-hole information**
   - Lund University's 2021 Master's thesis summary describes the conflict between Hawking evaporation and quantum unitarity, and reports significant theoretical progress from generalized entropy/Page-curve methods. Its scope is theoretical and includes idealized models; do not imply that every aspect of information recovery in realistic black holes is settled.
   - Sources: [Lund University — Recent Progress on the Black Hole Information Paradox](https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/publication/9060679); [NASA Science — Black Hole FAQs](https://science.nasa.gov/apod/frequently-asked-questions-faqs-on-black-holes/).

7. **Life beyond Earth**
   - NASA states that thousands of exoplanets are confirmed, while no evidence of life beyond Earth has been found. Habitable-zone location is a condition that could allow surface liquid water, not evidence of life; major variables in the Drake equation remain unknown.
   - Source: [NASA Exoplanet Exploration — Are We Alone?](https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanets/search-for-life/).

8. **Indus script**
   - Nature's article described the script as undeciphered in 2015. A BBC report from January 2025, interviewing University of Washington and TIFR researchers, confirms no accepted decipherment; it cites around 4,000 short inscriptions averaging about five signs and the absence of a bilingual text. Pattern-finding supports structure but does not verify a reading. The article copy avoids elevating unsupported claimed decipherments.
   - Sources: [BBC News — A million-dollar challenge to crack the script of early Indians (2025)](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c70q44zn18wo); [Nature — Ancient civilization: Cracking the Indus script (2015)](https://www.nature.com/articles/526499a).

9. **Cosmic magnetic-field origins**
   - The SKA Observatory states that fields are observed in interstellar gas, planets, stars, and galaxies but that their origin remains unknown. It frames primordial fields versus later generation and amplification as open alternatives, and summarizes radio methods including Faraday rotation, Zeeman splitting, and synchrotron emission.
   - Sources: [SKA Observatory — Understanding Cosmic Magnetism](https://www.skao.int/en/explore/science-goals/139/understanding-cosmic-magnetism); [Kavli Institute for Cosmology, Cambridge — Origin and role of magnetism](https://www.kicc.cam.ac.uk/research/formation-and-evolution-of-galaxies-and-supermassive-black-holes/origin-and-role-magnetism).

10. **Free will and determinism**
   - The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy explains the debate in terms of different accounts of control, alternative possibilities, sourcehood, responsibility, compatibilism, and incompatibilism. It also distinguishes empirical questions about human decision-making from the normative/metaphysical question of what counts as free will.
   - Sources: [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Free Will](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/freewill/); [Rakos et al. 2025, PMC — The Free Will: Determinism Debate in Contemporary Science and Philosophy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12162392/).

## Currency and scope caveats

- “Open” means no broadly accepted answer supported by the cited state of evidence, not that no progress or competing proposal exists.
- Dark-energy article states DESI's April 2026 full five-year analysis was expected in 2027; this deliberately avoids asserting results after the fetched institutional update.
- The latest fetched comprehensive Indus-script status report is from January 2025. It documents ongoing research and no accepted decipherment then; the article phrases the state as lack of scholarly consensus, not as a claim that no new proposal could ever appear.
- NASA's black-hole FAQ is an older background page, so the article's paradox framing is supported primarily by the Lund University thesis and limits itself to theoretical progress and unresolved complete description.
