# Between the Lines: Evidence Notes

Research checked against the full text of institutional pages, not search snippets. Notes below identify the factual basis used in the matching article; interpretations and reflections are original prose.

1. **The Book That Kept Eighteen Plays Alive — Shakespeare First Folio**
   - [Folger Shakespeare Library, “The Shakespeare First Folio”](https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeare-in-print/first-folio/)
   - Folger says the 1623 First Folio appeared seven years after Shakespeare's death, gathered 36 plays under comedy/history/tragedy, and preserved 18 plays not previously printed, including *Macbeth*, *The Tempest*, and *Twelfth Night*. It attributes compilation to actors and colleagues John Heminge and Henry Condell, and notes the folio format was a sturdier and more expensive large-book form than a quarto. Article's “might have vanished” is framed as possibility, as Folger itself says some might have been lost.

2. **Before Wonderland Had a Cheshire Cat — Alice manuscript**
   - [British Library, “Lewis Carroll’s Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland”](https://www.britishlibrary.cn/en/works/alice-in-wonderland/)
   - The British Library describes the story first being told to Alice Liddell and her sisters on a 1862 boat trip; Carroll wrote and illustrated *Alice's Adventures Under Ground* and gave it to Alice in 1864. For publication he expanded the tale, added two chapters and characters including the Hatter and Cheshire Cat, and used John Tenniel's illustrations. The article follows this documented manuscript-to-publication history.

3. **Frankenstein, Written in Layers — Mary Shelley manuscript**
   - [Shelley-Godwin Archive, “Frankenstein, or the Modern Prometheus”](https://shelleygodwinarchive.org/contents/frankenstein/)
   - The archive reports that the novel began with a now-lost story in summer 1816, that surviving draft notebooks from 1816–17 preserve 87% of the 1818 text, and that Mary Shelley and Percy Bysshe Shelley collaborated in producing the draft. It traces development from a two-volume draft to a three-volume fair copy and explains that only part of the latter survives. The article summarizes these points without assigning unsubstantiated proportions of authorship.

4. **Peter Rabbit Began as a Letter — Beatrix Potter**
   - [Victoria and Albert Museum, “Peter Rabbit: the tale of ‘The Tale’”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/peter-rabbit-the-tale-of-the-tale)
   - V&A dates Potter's illustrated letter to Noel Moore to 4 September 1893, says the story drew on her pet rabbit Peter Piper, and describes Potter borrowing and reworking the saved family letters for a book. It documents publisher rejections, Potter's privately printed black-and-white 1901 edition, and the commercial edition in 1902 after revisions. The article draws on this documented production path and the stated child-sized, affordable design intention.

5. **Jane Austen Before the Drawing Room — Austen juvenilia**
   - [British Library, “Jane Austen’s Juvenilia”](https://www.britishlibrary.cn/en/works/jane-austens-juvenilia/)
   - The British Library describes three surviving notebooks of Austen's early short works; earliest pieces probably date to 1786/87 and the latest dated entry to 1793. They circulated among family and friends rather than functioning as private diaries; physical wear suggests frequent reading. The page identifies *The History of England* as a satire written when Austen was 15 and notes sister Cassandra's illustrations. Article reflects the social circulation and satire.

6. **Proust’s Novel in the Margins — Proust proofs**
   - [Morgan Library & Museum, “Marcel Proust and Swann’s Way: 100th Anniversary”](https://www.themorgan.org/exhibitions/marcel-proust-and-swanns-way-100th-anniversary)
   - The Morgan describes an exhibition marking the 1913 publication of *Swann's Way*, first of seven volumes, with Proust's notebooks, preliminary drafts, galley proofs, other documents from the Bibliothèque nationale de France, and letters from the Morgan's collection. It says these materials offer insight into the creative process and the making of the work. The article's account of revision is a restrained inference from the presence of drafts and corrected proofs.

7. **The Tale That Became a Thousand-Year Conversation — *The Tale of Genji***
   - [The Metropolitan Museum of Art, “The Tale of Genji: A Japanese Classic Illuminated”](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2019/tale-of-genji)
   - The Met identifies Murasaki Shikibu as a lady-in-waiting in the early eleventh-century imperial court and presents the literary work's artistic legacy across the eleventh century to the present. Its exhibition included more than 120 works spanning painting, calligraphy, clothing, lacquer, ukiyo-e prints, and manga. Article describes that documented breadth as a continuing cultural conversation.

8. **A New Machine in an Old Book’s Clothing — Gutenberg Bible**
   - [Morgan Library & Museum, “The Gutenberg Bible”](https://www.themorgan.org/exhibitions/online/imperial-splendor/gutenberg-bible)
   - Morgan describes the Bible as printed at Mainz around 1455 using movable type, with an edition estimated at about 180 copies. Gutenberg and financial backer Johann Fust developed a press to produce multiple copies. Morgan notes the text imitated Gothic manuscript script and that much decoration was added by hand. The article's “threshold object” language is interpretation grounded in this juxtaposition of new printing and manuscript-style design.

9. **A Poem’s Many First Attempts — Langston Hughes and Harlem Renaissance**
   - [Library of Congress, “The Harlem Renaissance”](https://www.loc.gov/classroom-materials/harlem-renaissance/)
   - The Library of Congress places Langston Hughes among writers associated with the Harlem Renaissance/New Negro Movement and describes the period as diverse artistic activity, not a uniform group identity. Its teacher materials explicitly point to multiple drafts of Hughes's “The Ballad of Booker T” as evidence for studying writing and revision. The article uses both facts and avoids quoting or inventing draft changes.

10. **Middle-earth, Before It Was a Map in Your Mind — Tolkien archive**
    - [Bodleian Libraries, “Tolkien: Maker of Middle-earth” (archived exhibition page)](https://tolkien.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/)
    - The Bodleian page says the 2018 exhibition included draft manuscripts, illustrations and maps for *The Hobbit* and *The Lord of the Rings*, plus early abstract paintings, stories for Tolkien's children, rare personal objects, fan mail, and letters. Note: the page is now a Bodleian withdrawal notice/holding page, but retains the official exhibition description and dates.
    - [Bodleian Libraries, “Bodleian Online: Collection Stories”](https://visit.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/learning/collection-stories)
    - This current Bodleian page describes the libraries' collections and links to Tolkien exhibition material, identifying the exhibition as exploring the Tolkien archive. It is additional institutional context for the collection framing.
